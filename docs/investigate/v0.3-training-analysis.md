# v0.3 Training Analysis

## Overview

v0.3 represents a significant departure from v0.1 (baseline) and v0.2 (noisy CoT):
- **Dataset**: `kishanvavdara/nemotron-reasoning-traj` — 2,510 correctness-filtered training examples (vs 8,550 competition problems for v0.1)
- **Learning rate**: 1e-5 (20× lower than v0.1/v0.2's 2e-4)
- **Epochs**: 2 (vs 1 for v0.1/v0.2)
- **LoRA config**: `r=32, lora_alpha=32, target_modules=in_proj|out_proj|up_proj|down_proj` (regex vs all-linear)
- **Response format**: `{cot}\n</think>\n\boxed{answer}` (aligns with Nemotron pre-training)

## Training Metrics

![v0.3 Training Plot](../images/training_v03.png)

### Loss

- **Train loss**: 22.9 → 0.54 (epoch 0) → 0.49 (epoch 2 final)
  - Smooth, monotonic decrease across both epochs
  - No spikes or instability
  - Indicates clean data and appropriate learning rate

- **Eval loss**: 0.6521 (epoch 1) → **0.6336 (epoch 2)** ✓
  - Improved from epoch 1 to epoch 2 (best model saved at epoch 2)
  - Gap between train (0.49) and eval (0.634) suggests mild regularization / generalization
  - Much lower than v0.2-cot eval loss (1.305)

### Token Accuracy

- **Train**: ~80% → 84% over 2 epochs
- **Eval**: 83.04% (epoch 1) → 83.11% (epoch 2)
  - Plateau near epoch 1, very slight improvement at epoch 2
  - Indicates good token-level prediction (not ground-truth given at eval, but fed step-by-step)

### Grad Norm

- Flat and near-zero throughout (~0.004–0.006)
- **No spikes** — stable optimization trajectory
- Contrasts sharply with v0.2-cot (persistent noise) and v0.1-baseline (initial spike)
- Suggests the lr=1e-5 is well-tuned for this dataset and model

### Learning Rate Schedule

- Cosine annealing: 1e-5 → ~0.7e-8 by end of epoch 2
- Smooth decay following the cosine schedule
- No warmup issues visible

## Key Observations

### ✅ Strengths

1. **Training stability** — no gradient spikes, smooth loss curve
2. **Generalization** — eval loss improves epoch 1→2, no overfitting signal yet
3. **Data quality** — loss curves are clean (vs noisy CoT in v0.2)
4. **LR tuning** — 20× lower LR (1e-5) is appropriate for filtered CoT + long sequences

### ⚠️ Considerations

1. **Plateau at epoch 2** — token accuracy shows diminishing returns; could try 3 epochs to see if further improvement exists
2. **Small train set** — 2,510 examples is 30% of v0.1's competition data; risk of overfitting on longer horizon
3. **Different data distribution** — v0.3 uses CoT traces, not competition problem format; validation accuracy may not directly compare to v0.1/v0.2

## Comparison to Prior Runs

| Metric | v0.1-baseline | v0.2-cot | v0.3-filtered |
|--------|---------------|----------|---------------|
| Train data | 8,550 (competition) | 8,358 (peer CoT) | 2,510 (filtered CoT) |
| LR | 2e-4 | 2e-4 | 1e-5 |
| Epochs | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| Final train loss | 5.7 | 1.38 | 0.49 |
| Eval loss | 0.663 | 1.305 | **0.634** |
| Eval token acc | 80.64% | 67.54% | **83.11%** |
| Grad norm (final) | 1.75 | 0.042 | **0.0047** |
| Local val acc | 43.5% | 30.7% | *pending* |

**Story**: v0.2's regression (0.54 Kaggle vs 0.57 for v0.1) was driven by noisy CoT data and high learning rate → gradient spikes and poor generalization. v0.3 fixes both: cleaner data + much lower LR → stable training and better eval metrics. Whether this translates to higher Kaggle score depends on how well the CoT format (with `</think>` separator) aligns with actual generation behavior.
